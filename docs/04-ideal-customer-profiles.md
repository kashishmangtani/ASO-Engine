# 04 · Ideal Customer Profiles (ICPs)

**Rule:** pick **one or two** ICPs to start. A niche makes marketing, case studies and LinkedIn outreach far easier ("I help dental clinics get recommended by ChatGPT" beats "I help businesses with AI").

## Who is a good fit
A business is a strong fit when:
1. Its customers **research before buying** (they ask "which is best?" questions).
2. The **value of one new customer is high**, so a retainer pays back with a few extra customers.
3. It has **competitors that AI can name instead of it**.
4. It has a website it controls and someone who can approve changes.
5. It already spends money on marketing (SEO, ads or an agency).

## ICP A: High-value local service businesses
**Examples:** dental, orthodontic, cosmetic and medical clinics; physiotherapy; law firms; real-estate agencies; premium home services (renovation, solar, HVAC); wedding venues; private schools and tutoring centres.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Decision maker** | Owner, practice manager, managing partner |
| **Size** | 1–5 locations, 5–100 staff |
| **Customer value** | A single new patient or client is often worth a lot over their lifetime |
| **Typical AI questions** | "Best dentist in [city] for implants", "Is [competitor] good?", "Affordable family lawyer near me" |
| **Pains** | Rising ad costs; reviews and SEO don't feel like enough; competitors appear in ChatGPT answers; they don't know what AI says about them |
| **Triggers to buy** | A patient says "ChatGPT recommended you" (or a competitor); a new competitor opens; SEO results flattening; website redesign |
| **Objections** | "My customers don't use ChatGPT." "I already pay an SEO agency." "Can you guarantee results?" |
| **Answer** | Show them a live test: ask an AI assistant their buyers' questions on the call and see who it names |
| **Where to find them** | LinkedIn (owners and practice managers by city); local business groups; industry associations; Google Maps listings |

## ICP B: B2B SaaS and tech companies
**Examples:** SaaS from seed to growth stage, dev tools, fintech, HR tech, martech.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Decision maker** | Head of Marketing, Head of Growth, SEO or Content Lead, Founder (at early stage) |
| **Size** | 10–500 employees |
| **Typical AI questions** | "Best [category] software for [use case]", "[Competitor] alternatives", "[Tool A] vs [Tool B]" |
| **Pains** | Organic traffic falling as AI answers replace clicks; competitors appear in AI "alternatives" lists; leadership asks "what's our AI strategy?" |
| **Triggers** | Traffic drop; new board-level focus on AI; competitor launches comparison pages; fundraising (budget available) |
| **Objections** | "We have an in-house SEO team." "We'll buy a SaaS tool for this." |
| **Answer** | Position as an extra pair of hands plus measurement and a strategy layer that works *with* their SEO team; we implement, tools only report |
| **Where to find them** | LinkedIn Sales Navigator (titles + company headcount + industry "Software Development"); SaaS communities; product launch sites |

## ICP C: Marketing & web agencies (partner / white-label channel)
| | |
|---|---|
| **Decision maker** | Agency founder or owner, Head of SEO |
| **Why** | One partner can bring many end clients. They already have client trust but lack AI-search capability |
| **Offer** | White-label audits and monthly tracking reports under *their* brand (the platform already supports white-label branding). Revenue share or wholesale pricing |
| **Pains** | Clients asking "what about ChatGPT?"; fear of losing SEO retainers; no tooling |
| **Objections** | "We'll build it ourselves." "Will you steal our clients?" |
| **Answer** | Non-solicit agreement; it's faster to partner than to build; we stay invisible |
| **Where to find them** | LinkedIn (agency owners); agency communities; SEO conferences and meetups |

## ICP D: E-commerce / DTC brands
| | |
|---|---|
| **Decision maker** | Founder, Head of E-commerce, Marketing Manager |
| **Typical AI questions** | "Best [product] for [need]", "Is [brand] worth it?", "[brand] vs [brand]" |
| **Pains** | Paid acquisition is expensive; AI shopping answers favour well-reviewed, well-structured product data |
| **Focus** | Product schema, reviews, comparison and buying-guide content, presence on review sites |

## ICP E: Professional services (B2B)
**Examples:** accounting firms, consultancies, recruitment agencies, IT services, architects.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Decision maker** | Managing partner, Marketing Director, Business Development lead |
| **Pains** | Referral-dependent; buyers shortlist providers by asking AI |
| **Focus** | Expert authority content, author bios, case studies, third-party mentions |

## Disqualifiers (don't sell to)
- No website or no control over it
- Expects guaranteed #1 AI placement
- Brand-new business with no reviews and no content (start them with basics, or refer them on)
- Can't approve changes within weeks
